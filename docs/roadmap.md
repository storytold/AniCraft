# AniCraft roadmap

Principle: **infrastructure first, then high-traffic parity, then the long tail.** Every
milestone ends with a green tree: build, tests, clippy, a `--screenshot` check and an updated
"Honest status" section below.

## Honest status (2026-11-09, v0.1.0 early alpha)

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|
| Architecture | M0 skeleton in place: geom → doc → render → engine → cli → shell, layering enforced by `cargo test` (`src/architecture_tests.rs`: lower layers only, no UI crates below the shell, and the AGENTS.md map must match). |
| Rendering | CPU oracle with analytic AA, non-zero/even-odd, solid + linear/radial gradients, round-join strokes. **Mask clipping**: the display list carries nested clips; a clip is the anti-aliased union of its mask fills (strokes and opacity ignored), intersected with any enclosing clip, computed once per render band; an empty mask hides everything under it. **Group alpha**: a symbol instance below full opacity is a group in the display list; each render band composites the group's run of ops into its own buffer (nested groups stack) and blends it once at the group's alpha, so overlaps don't show through; SVG export writes `<g opacity>`. **Blend modes** (Animate's 14, on movie clip and button instances): an instance with one is an isolated group that blends at its close with the W3C separable formulas on premultiplied colour (Alpha and Erase keep or clear the backdrop); SVG gets CSS `mix-blend-mode`, Lottie the layer `bm`. **Bitmaps**: `Paint::Bitmap` fills (once or tiled) with bilinear sampling in premultiplied space, colour effects per sample; PNG/JPEG import to stage (a rectangle of the image's size); stored in the document as PNG (base64 in `.anicraft`), decoded once on first use; SVG export embeds each image once as a pattern. **Filters**: a filtered group is drawn on its own (by the same renderer, recursively, so nested filters work) into a buffer over its artwork grown by the filters' reach, filtered (box-blur passes; shadow and glow from the blurred, offset opacity; **bevel and gradient bevel** from the difference of the blurred opacity offset towards and away from the light, **gradient glow** from the glow's intensity, both through a colour ramp whose rest stop is pinned transparent, inner/outer/full and knockout, ADR 0025; colour matrix), and composited back as one image in its group, so alpha and blend apply; clips from inside the symbol apply before filtering and the instance's own clip after. **GPU stage, first slice** (M3, ADR 0019; `--features gpu-stage`, off by default): Preferences › Stage › Renderer *GPU (preview)* draws the stage with wgpu compute from the oracle's own prepared fills (tile binning with per-row backdrops on the CPU, exact area coverage and src-over per pixel on the GPU): solid and gradient fills and strokes, non-zero and even-odd; masks, group opacity, blend modes, filters and bitmaps fall back to the CPU per tile, and the status bar says so. Parity with the oracle: max 1/255, mean < 0.0001/255 on the sample, stress and analytic scenes (tests skip without an adapter). 4K with 1,000 shapes: 8.8–9.0 ms vs the CPU's 17.4–17.8 ms. **Second slice** (ADR 0029): on the window's wgpu device the stage tiles are drawn straight into textures egui shows (no readback; a headless device with readback when the window is on OpenGL); **masks** (nested, united artwork), **group opacity** and **all 14 blend modes** on the GPU, same parity (max 1/255, mean ≤ 0.0011/255); 4K: 1,000 shapes in 2.1–2.4 ms, 10k shapes in 10.2 ms as stage tiles, masked scenes 7× the CPU. **Third slice** (ADR 0032): **bitmap fills** (textures uploaded once per image and cached under a 256 MB budget, sampled with the oracle's own bilinear filter, colour effect and repeat rules: byte-identical) and **all seven filters** (each filtered symbol drawn into a layer of its own and filtered by compute passes that run the oracle's resolved steps: max 1/255, mean ≤ 0.007/255) on the GPU; the shaders now build with Direct3D's FXC, so DX12 windows get the GPU stage too (parity suite green on Vulkan and DX12); software adapters are refused (the CPU is faster), the start-up self-test compares with the oracle. **The GPU is the default stage renderer**: the shell's `gpu-stage` feature is on by default, Preferences › Stage › Renderer defaults to *Graphics card (GPU)*, and the CPU draws whenever there is no usable card, a tile is past the GPU's limits (more than 16 images, filters nested deeper than 4) or anything fails. 4K: the filtered sample in 3.9 ms (CPU 31.5), 200 bitmap fills in 4.4 ms (CPU 37.2). Not yet: sharing a filtered layer across a frame's tiles, more than 16 images per tile, GPU binning (ADR 0029), buffer reuse across tiles, GPU exports, a GPU runner in CI; the CPU stays the oracle and the export path. |
| Timeline | **Frame selection**: click, Shift-click or drag across frames and layers; F5/Shift+F5, F6/F7 and Shift+F6 (keys, buttons and right-click menu) act on the whole selection; drag selected keyframes left or right to move them, and up or down onto other layers (the source keeps empty frames, as in Animate) (with a drop outline; one undo step; keys they land on are replaced, and spans fill the frames left behind); removing or clearing selected frames that hold artwork asks first. **Cut/Copy/Paste Frames** (Ctrl+Alt+X/C/V, and plain Ctrl+X/C/V after clicking the timeline): own clipboard, copies cut exactly at the selection edges (mid-tween state kept), paste inserts like Animate, several layers paste onto the layers below (missing ones are created), fresh element ids, symbols travel with them. **Span length by dragging its end** (resize cursor, live outline with the length in frames and seconds; later frames move with it). Headless: `layers`/`count` on the frame commands, `timeline.move_keyframes`, `timeline.cut_frames/copy_frames/paste_frames`. Layers (show/lock/outline), keyframes, blank keyframes, insert/remove frames, classic tweens with 8 easing presets + curve preview, **shape tweens** (morph between any two shapes: segments made cubic, subpaths paired by size and grown from or shrunk to a point when counts differ, segment counts equalized by subdivision, windings matched and start points aligned; amber spans; classic ⇄ shape keeps the easing; **shape hints** (ADR 0023): Modify › Shape › Add Shape Hint (Ctrl+Shift+H) adds lettered markers `a`–`z` that the artist drags onto an edge on the first and last keyframe (rose until placed, amber placed on the first, mint on the last; right-click removes), the morph cuts both outlines at the hinted points and matches the pieces in order by length; `tween.add_hint/move_hint/remove_hint/clear_hints/hints`; not yet: View › Show Shape Hints, hints inside symbols edited in place), auto-key when editing inside a tween, frame labels, symbol nesting: graphics keep step with their timeline (loop, play once, single frame, first frame; set in Properties), movie clips loop their own timeline from when the instance appears (continuous across keyframes holding the same instance), buttons show their Up frame, onion skin (±2 frames), looping playback. **Mask layers**: right-click a layer › Mask turns it into a mask over the layer below (Masked adds more layers to it); masked layers are indented under a stencil icon; the mask applies on the stage and in every export alike, and while the mask layer is unlocked its edge shows in the layer colour so it can be picked and reshaped; masked-away parts cannot be picked; layers moved in under a mask join it, moved out leave it, and a mask moves with its whole group; masks inside symbols nest. Headless: `layer.toggle_mask`, `layer.set_type`. **Scenes** (ADR 0011): the movie is an ordered list of scenes, each its own timeline, edited one at a time (scene menu on the edit bar, Insert › Scene, Modify › Scene, Shift+PageUp/PageDown); add, duplicate (fresh ids), rename, reorder and remove (asks when the scene has artwork; undoable); PNG-sequence and GIF exports play every scene in order. **Layer folders**: nestable folders (depth ≤ 16) holding layers and masks through `Layer.parent`, one invariant (`Timeline::repair_masks`: a child sits below its parent inside the parent's unbroken run; broken links fall back to the nearest valid ancestor); hiding or locking a folder hides or locks its contents (rendering, picking, drawing); folders open and close on the timeline (view state); layers move in and out with Up/Down, a folder moves with its contents; deleting a folder deletes its contents (asks first). **Motion tweens** (ADR 0016): a span's object animated property by property (x, y, scale, rotation, skew, alpha, tint), each property its own curve of property keyframes with a per-segment ease (presets or a custom cubic Bézier, overshoot allowed); auto-key turns any edit of the object inside the span into property keys (every command, through the session), F6 keys every property, splits and copies keep the motion; sky-blue spans with property-key diamonds; **Motion Editor** tab beside the timeline: property list with values at the playhead, curve graph with draggable keys and Bézier handles, one undo step per drag. **Motion paths** (ADR 0030): a motion tween may carry a cubic path its object travels by arc length, timed by the X/Y curves (a straight path reproduces them exactly, overshoot included); shown on the stage in the motion colour with a dot per frame when the object or span is selected; Selection drags the path to bend it and its ends to move them, Subselection drags anchors and handles; moving the object reshapes the path; Orient to path and Straighten in Properties; splits cut the path. **Path points** (ADR 0038): with Subselection, double-click the path to add a point (shape and motion unchanged), Shift-click to select several anchors, Delete to remove them (the path refits through the rest; the ends stay), right-click for smooth/corner and Straighten; the Pen's Alt-click adds a point to a motion path or a selected shape; Ctrl-drag (⌘-drag) the path or its object, or edit Properties › Path start, to move the whole path with its timing exact. Headless: `timeline.create_motion_tween`, `motion.*` (`motion.set_path/clear_path/orient/add_point/delete_points/convert_point/move_path`). Not yet: anchors tied to frames (spatial keys, a model change: ADR 0038), a marquee over path anchors, nudging or transforming a whole path, retiming keys, rescaling keys with the span, XFL motion-tween import. **Camera layer** (ADR 0027): the camera button in the timeline header or the Camera tool (C) adds a Camera layer on top; its frame shows on the stage in orange; drag to pan, turn with the round handle, wheel to zoom (one undo step per drag); Properties › Camera (position, zoom, rotation, tint, reset); keyframes and classic or motion tweens animate it; layer Depth gives pan parallax; View › Preview Through Camera shows the movie's picture (artwork tools pause there); hiding the camera layer turns it off; every export, Test Movie and the GPU stage see it. Headless: `camera.add/remove/set/info`, `layer.set_depth`. Not yet: editing through the preview, perspective depth, XFL camera import. **Motion guides**: guided layers (children of a guide through `Layer.parent`) have their classic tweens follow the guide's first path by arc length, from the point nearest each start keyframe to the point nearest its end (`geom::walk::PathWalk`), optionally orienting to the path; guide artwork is listed for picking and editing, drawn on the stage as a line, and never rendered or exported. |

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| GPU and CPU renderers drift apart | Golden parity tests in CI; CPU stays the oracle. |
| XFL edge encoding is under-documented | Build a self-authored corpus; unknown elements kept verbatim; fail with readable errors. |
| egui immediate-mode cost with big timelines | Virtualise the frame grid (paint only visible cells); cache layer row summaries per revision. |
| Snapshot undo memory | `Arc` structural sharing in M2; history size cap with a preference. |
